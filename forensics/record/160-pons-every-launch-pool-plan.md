# Pons every-launch quote pools: discovery, ranges, and gas

The proposed system is distinct from the [NOTHINGBURGER counterfactual replay](150-nothingburger-counterfactual-replay.md). It would queue each newly launched Pons token `X`, open an independent Uniswap v4 `X/Q` pool when a viable **quote-only** range is available, start with **one strategy-owned LP position** (the earlier three-range idea remains a parameter sweep), and exit at the first one-sided boundary after the position has entered its band. It then liquidates `X`, burns recovered quote token `Q`, and distributes ETH. No such strategy was deployed or traded here.

## How to find the launches

Pons V2 begins on a bonding curve. The factory `0x7eD598BcEf8bd9Edd8C97A195C6d13f40801EC7e` emits `TokenLaunched(token, curve, deployer, pairToken, launchConfigId, graduationThreshold)` at creation. A Pons-hook `PoolRegistered` event and a v4 `Initialize` happen only at **graduation**, too late for a launch-time strategy. The factory exposes `getLaunchedToken(token)` to verify a *supplied* address; it stores launches in a mapping and exposes no iterable token list. The hook and v4 PoolManager likewise have mappings and events, not an onchain linked list of launches. A contract cannot read past EVM logs. [Pons event guide](https://docs.ponsfamily.com/v2#events-to-index), [factory source](https://github.com/ponsdotdev/pons-labs/blob/main/contractsV2/src/v2/PonsV2LaunchFactory.sol), [PoolManager source](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/main/src/PoolManager.sol).

Use a block-cursor watcher of the factory's `TokenLaunched` logs, with websocket notification and `eth_getLogs` backfill after reconnect. The watcher's **only write** is `enqueue(token)` signed by the owner; the contract verifies `getLaunchedToken(token).exists` and deduplicates it. Put fresh launches on a **LIFO entry stack**, so the next transfer works on the newest token. The safe production stages are `QUEUED → FUNDED → ENTERED_RANGE → EXIT_READY → CLOSED`, with pool initialization **and** initial LP mint in one atomic `FUNDED` step; an empty initialized pool can acquire a stale or manipulated price before a later mint. A position does **not** exit merely because it starts quote-only. Once the pool is observed trading inside the range, an observed one-sided boundary (all `Q` or all `X` principal, excluding accrued fees) makes it exit-ready. **No custom v4 hook is used**: a permissionless inspector/keeper must read the live v4 price and position ticks, report entry/boundary state to the cursor, and execute the exit. An ERC-20 `_transfer` cannot identify which v4 pool traded from its sender/recipient alone. Polling or reacting to `Swap` logs can miss a transient boundary that reverses before the inspection transaction, so without a hook the enforceable rule is the **first observed** boundary, not necessarily the first intra-block touch. Each eligible **direct EOA** quote-token transfer with enough gas attempts **one** stage, prioritizing a ready exit before popping the latest entry; Q transfers inside routers/v4 settlement are excluded to avoid nested pool operations. A failed stage stays retryable and must not permanently block later launches. A permissionless `processNext()` provides a fallback if transfers stop. There is **no time-based exit** in this rule.

An ERC-20 transfer cannot discover a launch that was never enqueued. It also pays for its own attempted stage: creating a pool and LP position can cost hundreds of thousands of gas, making ordinary token transfers expensive. Calls into the v4 position manager can themselves transfer `Q`; the token needs a strict in-progress guard to prevent recursive stage execution. Transfers occurring inside another v4 settlement may be unable to open a nested v4 operation, so stage failure should be caught without reverting the user's transfer. At most **5,767 opens plus 5,767 exits per day** would need triggering transactions if every launch entered and reached a boundary; most launches may never meet the quote-only entry trigger. A lower transfer rate builds a backlog.

## Token and range mechanics

The quote `Q` in this design is **our custom ERC-20**, deployed separately from Pons. The Pons launch tokens are the opposing `X` assets, not the quote. The `_transfer` cursor can therefore live in `Q`; there is no Pons creator-fee escrow for `Q`. The user intends to launch `Q` through Pools.xyz. Its ordinary UI creates its fixed token implementation, but the [underlying Uniswap LiquidityLauncher](https://github.com/Uniswap/liquidity-launcher/blob/main/docs/DeploymentGuide.md) also supports **an existing custom ERC-20** by atomically batching `depositToken` and `distributeToken` in one `multicall`. For its [Instant Launch strategy](https://github.com/Uniswap/liquidity-launcher/blob/main/docs/TechnicalReference.md), `Q` must have exactly **1 billion fixed units with 18 decimals**, the whole supply is distributed into a permanently locked **Q/ETH** pool on Robinhood, and that pool has a fixed **0.25% LP fee** with no hook. The custom `_transfer` must preserve exact standard ERC-20 transfers; a transfer tax or rebase is incompatible with the launcher. The entire initial `Q` supply is committed to the locked launch position; the vault must **acquire valuable `Q`** for later `X/Q` positions, for example through the Q/ETH market. This is the same finite capital budget as a direct ETH-funded strategy, not free LP inventory. The Pools.xyz UI does not expose this existing-token launch path, and listing of a directly launched token in its frontend is unverified. The independent, withdrawable `X/Q` positions use [Uniswap v4 PoolManager and PositionManager](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/create-pool), owned by the strategy vault. Pools.xyz Instant Launch does not create those custom-pair positions or give them its fixed launch-pool terms.

For the currently observed Pons curve terms (1.68 ETH phantom quote, 4.2 ETH graduation threshold), the theoretical graduation *spot* is **12.25×** the initial spot. The proposed upper targets are therefore 12.25×, 24.5×, and 122.5× the launch spot, before repricing for movements in `Q/ETH`. `getReserves()` and fee/snipe-tax state determine executable curve quotes; the marginal ratio alone is not a swap quote. [Pons curve guide](https://docs.ponsfamily.com/v2#getting-a-quote).

The funding side is decisive. If v4 price is `Q per X`, then at the **lower boundary** of an upward `[launch, graduation]` position, the position is **X-only**. As `X` rises *through a fairly priced band*, buyers take `X` from the position and pay `Q`; the LP converts from `X` to `Q`. For example, normalize the launch spot to 1 Q/X and the graduation spot to 12.25 Q/X: at spot 1, a `[1, 12.25]` band needs `X`, while at spot 12.25 it can be funded entirely with `Q`. The vault therefore seeds **Q-only** only after the executable external `X/Q` price has climbed to the chosen range's upper bound; if price later falls through the band, traders sell `X` into it and receive `Q`, so the vault acquires `X`. Initializing an independent pool at 12.25 while Pons still sells `X` around 1 creates an overbid for `X` if `Q` has an external value: arbitrageurs can sell cheap `X` into that pool and take the vault's `Q`. The intended rise before a fall does not earn LP fees while the position remains out of range above its upper bound. Each of the three originally proposed upper bounds (graduation, 2× graduation, 10× graduation) would need its own later activation trigger. A token that never reaches the bound stays queued without LP gas. Minting `Q` freely does not itself give it an ETH price or create arbitrage demand. An independently initialized `X/Q` pool needs an executable external `Q/ETH` price to set sensible ticks; the live relative price is `X/ETH` divided by `Q/ETH`, so the two tokens merely starting at similar ETH prices is not enough to keep the ratio fixed. Neither swap activity nor a fee automatically guarantees positive ETH proceeds after inventory changes, burning, and gas. [Uniswap liquidity overview](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/liquidity/overview).

Uniswap v4 technically allows an `X/Q` **5–50% static LP fee** (`fee` 50,000–500,000 in millionths). There is **no custom v4 hook** in this design. The `Q` contract records outcomes from prior `X/Q` pools, chooses a fee for the **next newly initialized pool**, and passes that fixed fee to the executor; it never changes an existing pool's fee. Keep the selected fee stable across retries for the same token. Use randomized exploration across 5–50%, with more future selections near fees whose prior pools produced better ETH-valued net outcomes. Feedback must include realized ETH proceeds, valued remaining inventory, gas, and **actual swap volume**, not just nominal fee percentage. Positions still open or with zero trading cannot silently disappear from the learning sample: track them as censored/idle observations at a measurement horizon without forcing an LP exit. The locked Pools.xyz `Q/ETH` launch pool remains fixed at 0.25%. A seller paying `X` into the custom `X/Q` pool also pays its LP fee in `X`, which may collapse in ETH value. At an opening pool price of 12.25 Q/X against an external price of 1 Q/X, even a 50% fee leaves a first marginal sale worth about 6.125 Q per X before price impact, so it does not remove the initial arbitrage. High fees can prevent later trades entirely, leaving zero fee income. [Uniswap v4 fee units](https://github.com/Uniswap/v4-core/blob/main/src/libraries/LPFeeLibrary.sol), [fee accrual](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/fees).

The [static next-pool fee prototype](../launch_cursor/StaticNextPoolFee.sol) uses 46 integer-percent choices from 5% through 50%, explores uniformly on 15% of selections, and otherwise chooses the highest observed mean ETH-valued net return. It stores one chosen fee per `X`, so open retries cannot resample it. An executor may report one bounded outcome only **after** a successful exit. This is a feedback mechanism, not a calibrated optimizer: pool outcomes differ by token and time, open positions may be right-censored, and EVM pseudo-randomness is biasable. Before production, compare randomized fee arms on matched entry conditions, include idle/stuck positions in evaluation, and verify that higher fees actually increase net proceeds.

## Gas-aware LP fee claims

There are two **LP** fee streams, and no Pons quote-token creator fee. The locked Pools.xyz `Q/ETH` launch position can earn 0.25% fees; its [FeeSplitter](https://github.com/Uniswap/liquidity-launcher/blob/main/docs/TechnicalReference.md) collects and routes the configured beneficiary's share, which the beneficiary then claims. Separately, swaps through the active range of each withdrawable `X/Q` position accrue fees in `X` and/or `Q`. For those positions, the vault can claim accrued fees without reducing principal liquidity by calling PositionManager with `DECREASE_LIQUIDITY` for **zero** liquidity followed by `TAKE_PAIR`. At the one-sided boundary exit, full withdrawal claims any fees left, so interim claims are an optimization. [Uniswap fee guide](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/position-manager#collecting-fees), [Pools.xyz fee collection](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/liquidity/liquidity-launchpad/guides/collect-and-compound-fees), [fee accrual](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/fees#in-range-liquidity-and-fee-accrual).

The cursor should service **ready exits first, fresh LIFO entries second, fee harvests third**. The permissionless position inspector may flag an active position for LP-fee harvest after swaps. Before collecting LP fees, the executor should estimate collectable `X` and `Q`, convert both using executable ETH routes, and require their gross ETH value to exceed a conservative multiple of `estimated harvest gas × tx.gasprice` plus any conversion/distribution costs. A claim below that threshold remains pending and can accumulate more fees. A real executor must measure the actual fee-token balances received, handle both denominations, and account for extra gas if it immediately swaps/distributes them. Using `Q`'s nominal minted value instead of an executable quote would make this gate meaningless. Fee harvests never outrank a boundary exit, and a failed harvest must not block later launches.

An interim `X/Q` fee claim is an extra transaction: those fees would also be collected at the boundary exit. The reason to claim sooner is to sell volatile `X` fees before they fall, distribute earned value sooner, or reuse `Q`. Compare that benefit with the **incremental** claim, conversion, and payout gas, rather than counting the entire accrued fee value as new profit. The locked `Q/ETH` launch LP has no boundary exit, so its beneficiary fee share should be collected/claimed whenever the claimable ETH-equivalent amount clears the full collection and claim gas plus a margin. The exact thresholds and gas ceilings need RH receipt measurements. The custom `Q` token itself has no transfer tax; that is required for launcher compatibility.

## Exit and distribution

The proposed proceeds rule is: withdraw all strategy positions; sell half of recovered `X` for ETH and half for `Q`; burn the `Q` obtained from the sale and any returned `Q`; split the resulting ETH equally between the wizard fanout and developer. On that definition, each recipient gets **25% of gross `X` liquidation value**, while the other 50% is spent buying and burning `Q`, before fees, slippage, and gas. Route `X` to its live Pons curve while it is open and to its graduated v4 pool after phase 2; the temporary swept phase must wait or retry. Place minimum-output and deadline bounds on both sales.

Squarefun's current Robinhood wizard fanout is `0x1b88A6c6516FD2918905186F21Bb9F5CaA1a15c8`. Its [verified `FeeFanout` source/ABI](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/4663/0x1b88A6c6516FD2918905186F21Bb9F5CaA1a15c8?fields=abi,sources) accepts ERC-20 balances for harvest/claim and has no payable ETH receiver. Wrap the wizard half of the ETH to WETH and transfer WETH to that contract; send the developer half as ETH to a configured recipient. Reverify the fanout address from the live Squarefun frontend before deployment.

## One-day scale and sampled transaction cost

In the exact 24-hour replay window (Robinhood blocks **77,940,490–78,796,050**), the factory emitted **5,767 `TokenLaunched` events**, about **4.00 launches/minute**: 4,628 paired with native ETH, 228 with USDG, and 911 with other ERC-20 assets. Only **52** Pons pools graduated during that window; that is a different, much smaller event stream. A literal open-at-every-launch rule would request **5,767 pools plus either 5,767 or 17,301 LP positions per day**, regardless of whether anyone later trades them. Under quote-only upper-bound gating, 5,767 is the **enqueue count**, not the funded LP count; the funded cadence requires a separate price-path replay. Non-ETH launch pairs require an additional executable path to ETH for the proposed payout. [Launch cadence data](../results/pons-v2-launch-cadence-24h.json).

In that same window, **42 of the 52 graduations were ETH-paired**. If the first Q-only band is armed only when an ETH-paired X reaches its graduation reference, the observed candidate ceiling is **42 positions/day** (1.75/hour on average, five in the busiest hour), with a lower bound of zero if none passes the executable-price, capital, and range checks. Thirty-eight of those 42 tokens launched inside the window; four launched earlier. This ceiling applies to that graduation-gated ETH-only rule, not to every possible X/Q configuration: a moving Q/ETH rate or a different upper tick could create earlier or later opportunities. It is **not** a fee-income or profit estimate. [Birth and graduation cadence data](../results/pons-birth-cadence-24h.json).

One observed zero-hook ETH-pair [pool creation and one LP mint transaction](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/tx/0x863453b8e1918868ab51fcf659389cd771cc80d786dda25238eddd2bc34294de) used **417,375 gas** at **0.034794 gwei**, or **0.00001452214575 ETH**. Multiplying that one receipt by 5,767 gives **0.08375 ETH ($217.75 at $2,600/ETH)** for openings in the literal open-every-launch case. The median of ten sampled standalone LP removals used **149,821 gas** at a **0.03213 gwei** median price; multiplying gives another **0.02776 ETH ($72.18)** if every position later reaches a boundary and exits. Combined, about **0.11151 ETH ($289.93)** for one-position open/close in that maximal case. This is an illustrative historical-price extrapolation, not a gas estimate for a custom vault or the gated strategy's expected bill.

For comparison, 20 separate [empty-pool initializations](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/tx/0x475e7fcaf593c5225571361010809b27a3b07c61268ea030cf9c5122e245a785) had median **51,844 gas** and **0.000001636 ETH** paid. At the observed cadence, opening empty pools alone extrapolates to **$24.53/day**. Combining that median with unrelated standalone later-add and removal samples suggests roughly **$234/day** for split init/add/exit. It is not directly comparable to the combined receipt and does not price the user's executor. Splitting init from mint solely to save gas risks minting at a stale pool price.

For three positions, combining that initial pool-plus-one-position receipt with two median standalone mints and three median removals gives roughly **0.28321 ETH ($736.34)** for one day of opens/closes. Batching three mints or burns could change the gas materially. Neither estimate includes contract deployment, launch indexing/queue calls, quote-token acquisition, swaps, wrapping WETH, or payout execution. The one-position combined-receipt estimate requires about **$0.0503 net ETH per launch on average** to cover sampled opening/withdrawal gas at the *system* level. When a quote-token transfer triggers the cursor, the **transfer's transaction sender** pays that step's gas; the owner wallet does not necessarily pay it. The owner still pays gas for `enqueue` and supplies LP capital, while steps that receive insufficient transfer gas remain pending. Moving gas to transfer users changes who pays, not the total cost or the need for valuable pool inventory.

## Current prototype and missing evidence

The [hookless contract prototype](../launch_cursor/README.md) now contains the fixed-supply Q token and LIFO scheduler, a 5–50% **static fee selector for the next X/Q pool**, a vault that atomically initializes and mints one Q-only v4 position, a same-transaction Pons/Q-ETH spot-price guard, and a permissionless live-price inspector. `enqueue(X)` records every verified Pons launch (including a fast graduation) but does not arm an open; a separate automated price keeper must submit a short-lived `configureOpen(X, config)` after verifying an executable X/Q price and Q budget. A Q transfer or `processNext()` can then attempt that configured open, if the matching price guard is bound. Five local integration tests cover an unconfigured launch staying dormant, fast graduation enqueue, a whole-range jump triggering exit readiness, a return to the quote boundary after an interior observation, and emergency abort of a stuck position. A **read-only Robinhood fork test** initialized real zero-hook Uniswap v4 pools, minted Q-only positions, then burned them and recovered Q in both token-address orderings using the owner emergency path. It used a mock Pons factory and mock guard. This verifies v4 mint and burn mechanics, **not** real Q/X market pricing or any realized outcome. No live transaction was broadcast.

The real executor's `previewHarvest` and `exit` intentionally revert. It cannot yet value accrued X/Q fees in ETH, liquidate X through the correct active/swept/graduated route, burn recovered Q, pay the wizard fanout and developer, or return a trustworthy net result to the fee selector. Its normal mechanical `withdrawPosition` is not reachable through Q. The owner can call `emergencyAbort(X)` to burn an active LP at any price and recover X/Q without automatic payout, but that is not the requested autonomous exit. Standalone active-curve and graduated-v4 X-to-ETH sale adapters passed read-only Robinhood fork tests with real Pons liquidity, but are not connected to the executor. The current contracts **must not custody live LP capital**. A selected PoolKey can also be initialized first by someone else, causing that X to fail and eventually require a skip. With no completed exit, the 5–50% selector explores every assignment and has no realized-return feedback. These are deployment blockers, not evidence of profitability or a tested 5–50% optimum.

For an ETH-paired Pons X, live curve reserves (phase 0) or the graduated Pons v4 pool price (phase 2) and Q/ETH launch-pool price can form a **marginal** X/Q reference. [OpenPriceGuard](../launch_cursor/OpenPriceGuard.sol) checks a configured starting price against that reference in the same transaction as mint, within a deployment-set bound capped at 10% and with active Q/ETH liquidity; swept phase 1 waits. These spots are not executable trade quotes: size, Pons taxes, Q/ETH pool fee, tick depth, and possible spot manipulation matter. An offchain preflight saved in the config transaction can go stale before the later transfer-triggered open; the deadline and spot check limit drift, but do not solve same-block manipulation or price impact. No size-aware Q/ETH oracle or guaranteed executable-price bound is implemented. [Pons quote formulas](https://docs.ponsfamily.com/v2#getting-a-quote), [Uniswap v4 quoting guide](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/sdks/v4/guides/swapping/quoting), [current InstantLaunch strategy](https://github.com/Uniswap/liquidity-launcher/blob/main/src/strategies/InstantLaunchStrategy.sol).

The historical **5,767 launches/day** is discovery cadence, not expected funded entries. A funded-entry cadence or ETH-valued profit forecast for this hookless Q-only rule needs a replay of *both* X/ETH and Q/ETH executable prices, the chosen tick bands and fee arms, in-pool swap volume, inventory at observed boundary, and full liquidation gas. The earlier [19-signal NOTHINGBURGER replay](150-nothingburger-counterfactual-replay.md) launched a Pons quote token, used a 30-minute exit, and modeled synthetic pools; its positive cell cannot be transferred to this custom-Q boundary-exit system.
